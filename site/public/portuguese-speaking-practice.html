Put the Portuguese you know into conversation

Portuguese speaking practice is especially useful when you understand the topic but need time to put a response together. A simple situation helps turn familiar words into a question, explanation or decision.

Use familiar words in a new answer

Practise words you already know while asking for directions, describing an object, or reacting to a suggestion. The setting gives you clues without giving you the whole answer.

Build from simple to longer conversations

Beginners can practise clear, short patterns. Intermediate learners and students can connect past and present events, explain their reasons, and handle an unexpected follow-up.

Prepare for travel, work and daily life

A simple Portuguese directions conversation

This Portuguese exchange uses common forms that remain understandable across many settings and leaves room to ask about distance or landmarks.

Diálogo: encontrar a estação

Tutor: Bom dia. Em que posso ajudar?

Estudante: Onde fica a estação, por favor?

Tutor: Claro. Siga em frente e vire à direita.

Estudante: Entendi. É longe daqui?

Correction: matching words in the plural

Try again: «Eu gosto muito de aprender novas idioma.»

Corrected: «Eu gosto muito de aprender novos idiomas.»

In Portuguese, idiomas is masculine plural, so the correct form is novos idiomas.

Widen the conversation from A1 to B2

At each CEFR level, you can connect more information and respond more freely when the conversation changes direction.

A1

Use greetings, numbers and short phrases to ask about places, prices and immediate needs.

A2

Describe routines and recent activities, link basic details and make straightforward arrangements.

B1

Tell a connected story, explain a practical problem and suggest what should happen next.

B2

Compare alternatives, explain an opinion in more detail, and take part in a longer discussion.

Use Portuguese across the situations that shape a day

Try the same language in different situations so it becomes easier to use outside one familiar dialogue.

Travel

Check a departure point, ask a host about public transport, or explain that a booking confirmation has not arrived.

Work

Clarify who owns a task, present a short progress update, or propose a new time for a discussion.

Study

Ask about course materials, compare notes after a lecture, or outline the main point of a group presentation.

Restaurants and cafés

Ask what comes with a dish, order for the table, respond to a suggestion, or request the bill.

Everyday services

Collect an order, ask about a repair, explain what you need in a shop, or check when a service closes.

People and plans

Arrange a walk, discuss a shared hobby, or tell someone what you enjoyed about a recent weekend.

Portuguese practice questions

These answers can help you choose a level, a practice mode, and a useful everyday topic.

Can I begin Portuguese speaking practice with limited vocabulary?

Yes. Choose A1 and a simple task such as greeting someone, buying an item, or asking where a place is. A complete short conversation is a useful starting point.

What kind of Portuguese examples should I practise?

Begin with clear everyday phrases for common situations. Change the setting and details so that you learn to shape your own response instead of depending on one script.

How can I use a correction effectively?

Find the exact change, such as words that need to match in the plural, then create another sentence with a different noun or verb. Use it again while the topic is still fresh.

Can I alternate between voice and text?

Yes. Text makes endings and spelling easier to check, while voice asks you to answer more immediately. In both modes, the next question follows the conversation.

How do I keep Portuguese conversation practice varied?

Rotate a guided lesson, a travel task, a work or study conversation, and an everyday social topic. The 100+ lesson library and selectable AI tutors give you plenty of ways to change the subject.
