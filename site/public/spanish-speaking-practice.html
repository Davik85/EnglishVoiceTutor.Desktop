Spanish practice for the words that disappear in conversation

You may recognise a past tense in a story yet hesitate when someone asks what happened yesterday. Regular Spanish speaking practice helps familiar words come more easily when you need them.

Use words you already recognise

Bring familiar Spanish into a small problem, a description, or a follow-up answer. A clear situation makes those words easier to remember than an isolated list.

Practise at the right level

Beginners can build reliable question-and-answer patterns. Intermediate learners and students can connect events, express doubt, and add detail when the tutor asks why.

Prepare for travel and working life

Travellers can practise requests they may need the same day. Workers can prepare updates and polite disagreements. Learners without regular conversation partners can create more chances to speak.

Set a scene, then see where the Spanish goes

Choose Spanish, your CEFR level, a topic or practical situation, and an AI tutor. Reply by voice or text. Each new question follows the conversation and helps the situation develop naturally.

Give yourself a role

Be a customer comparing two jackets, a student planning a presentation, or a visitor asking about a local bus. A role makes the words immediately useful.

Respond to new information

The AI Spanish tutor can add a new detail, ask for a reason, or offer another option. You practise adapting instead of predicting every line in advance.

Use the correction straight away

Read the corrected sentence and notice the key change. Then continue the topic and try to use that word ending, verb form, or linking word again.

A shopping exchange with room to continue

The learner in this Spanish dialogue states a need, answers a size question, and leaves several possible follow-ups about colour, price or fit.

Diálogo: buscar ropa para un viaje

Tutor: Buenas tardes, ¿busca algo en particular?

Estudiante: Sí, necesito una chaqueta ligera para el viaje.

Tutor: ¿Qué talla usa?

Estudiante: Normalmente uso una M.

Correction: a finished time period

Try again: «Ayer he comprado una chaqueta.»

Better in this context: «Ayer compré una chaqueta.»

With ayer, compré is the natural choice here for an action that was completed yesterday.

Move from essential Spanish to connected ideas

CEFR levels let the same theme grow from a short everyday exchange into a flexible conversation with reasons, alternatives and detail.

A1

Ask and answer about identity, location, prices, food and simple daily needs.

A2

Link short statements about routines, recent events, preferences and immediate plans.

B1

Narrate a sequence, explain why something matters and cope with a change to the original plan.

B2

Explore advantages and drawbacks, soften disagreement, and keep a longer conversation going while explaining your point clearly.

Use Spanish in situations that matter to you

Useful Spanish goes well beyond holidays. Change the people, place and problem so you can practise giving a fresh answer.

Travel

Ask whether a museum ticket includes an exhibition, explain a missed connection, or check the last bus back to your accommodation.

Work

Introduce a client call, report what has been completed, or agree who will handle the next task.

Study

Choose a presentation topic, compare two sources, or ask a classmate how they reached a different conclusion.

Restaurants and cafés

Order several dishes to share, ask about an ingredient, or explain politely that an item has not arrived.

Everyday shopping

Compare sizes, ask about a return, describe the item you need, and react when the first option is unavailable.

Plans and relationships

Invite a friend to an event, suggest a different time, or talk about a family tradition and why you value it.

Spanish conversation practice questions

Start with a clear situation, then leave enough room to make your own choices.

Which Spanish level should I select first?

Choose the level at which you can finish a short exchange with some effort. If every answer is already easy, move up; if you cannot build a basic response, choose a simpler situation or move down.

Can I practise Spanish for both travel and work?

Yes. Use a service situation for direct requests, then switch to a workplace conversation that asks you to explain, plan, or disagree politely. Each setting uses Spanish differently.

How can I remember a Spanish correction?

Notice the smallest meaningful change, say or write a new sentence with it, and use it again later in the dialogue. A correction is most useful when it helps you say something new.

Does text mode still count as conversation practice?

Yes. It gives you more time to check accents, word endings and verb choices while the conversation continues from your earlier replies. Use voice when you want to answer more immediately.

What if I have nobody nearby who speaks Spanish?

You can practise without finding a conversation partner. Choose a topic and AI tutor whenever convenient, then use the guided lessons to keep your sessions varied.
