Use German when it is your turn to answer

German speaking practice helps with the moment between understanding a question and building a reply. Regular conversations make it easier to use grammar and vocabulary without planning every sentence in advance.

Bring familiar words into conversation

If you understand more words than you use, a clear role and goal can help you bring verbs, nouns and linking phrases to mind and put them into complete sentences.

Build longer answers step by step

Beginners can practise introductions, directions and purchases. Intermediate learners and students can describe a process, explain a choice, and join several ideas in one answer.

Prepare for travel and work

Travellers can rehearse transport or accommodation problems. Workers can practise meetings and formal requests. Anyone short of regular German conversation practice can create more chances to speak.

Build the conversation one answer at a time

Select German, choose your CEFR level, decide on a topic or situation, and pick an AI tutor. Speak your response or type it. The next question follows what you have said, so a meeting can move from choosing a time to agreeing an agenda.

Choose how formal to be

A workplace request may call for Sie, while a conversation with a friend can use du. The situation helps you practise the right tone as well as the vocabulary.

Keep the conversation connected

The AI German tutor follows the developing conversation. You can ask a follow-up, reject a suggestion politely, or add missing information instead of working through unrelated drills.

Learn from one clear correction

Compare your sentence with the corrected version. Notice one useful change—perhaps verb placement or a word ending—then try another answer with the same pattern.

A German meeting arranged in four turns

This exchange practises availability, the common time expression halb zehn, and a clear agreement between colleagues.

Dialog: eine Besprechung planen

Tutor: Haben Sie morgen Zeit für eine kurze Besprechung?

Lernende Person: Ja, morgen Vormittag passt es mir gut.

Tutor: Wie wäre es um halb zehn?

Lernende Person: Das passt. Ich schicke gleich eine Einladung.

Correction: putting the verb after Morgen

Learner: „Morgen ich fahre mit dem Zug zur Arbeit.“

Better: „Morgen fahre ich mit dem Zug zur Arbeit.“

When the sentence starts with Morgen, the verb comes next. Here, fahre moves before ich.

Let each CEFR level change the task

Progress in German means gaining room to connect ideas, repair a sentence and respond to an unexpected detail.

A1

Give basic personal information and complete short everyday exchanges with familiar phrases.

A2

Describe routines, appointments and past activities in linked but uncomplicated sentences.

B1

Explain a problem, narrate what happened and suggest a practical solution with reasons.

B2

Develop a viewpoint, respond to objections and vary sentence structure through a longer conversation.

Use German where a precise response matters

Move between formal and informal situations so that your German conversation practice is useful beyond one familiar script.

Travel

Ask about a missed rail connection, confirm where to validate a ticket, or report an item left at reception.

Work

Set priorities for the week, explain a technical delay in plain language, or request feedback on a draft.

Study

Arrange library work, compare research topics, or ask a seminar leader to clarify the next step.

Restaurants and cafés

Reserve a table, ask whether a meal contains a particular ingredient, and divide an order among friends.

Everyday administration

Ask about opening hours, describe a parcel-delivery problem, or make an appointment for a service.

Personal conversation

Talk about commuting, recommend a place in your city, or discuss how a new habit has changed your week.

German practice: common questions

Choose a situation you can complete, then add more detail as your confidence grows.

Do I need strong German grammar before I start?

No. At A1 or A2, begin with a simple situation and short sentences. The conversation gives each grammar point a practical use, and a correction shows what to review.

Can I practise formal and informal German?

Yes. Choose situations where the relationship is clear—a service encounter, colleague, classmate or friend—and pay attention to whether the conversation uses Sie or du.

How does text mode help with German word order?

Text shows the whole sentence at once. You can check where the main verb and time phrase appear, then return to voice and try another answer.

What if I forget a noun during a German conversation?

Describe what it does, ask for the word, or say the idea another way. Keeping the conversation moving is a useful skill at B1 and B2.

How can I use the 100+ guided lessons?

Choose a guided lesson when you want clear steps, then use the same words in a different open conversation. A new situation helps you find out what you can use without prompts.
