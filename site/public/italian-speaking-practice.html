A simple Italian restaurant conversation

This conversation practises a reservation name, a polite welcome, and a question that could lead into seating or ordering.

Dialogo: arrivare al ristorante

Tutor: Buonasera, avete prenotato?

Studente: Sì, abbiamo un tavolo a nome Rossi.

Tutor: Perfetto. Preferite stare vicino alla finestra?

Studente: Sì, volentieri, grazie.

Correction: matching subject and verb

Try again: «Mi piace molto i piatti regionali.»

Corrected: «Mi piacciono molto i piatti regionali.»

Because i piatti regionali is plural, Italian uses piacciono here.

Do more with Italian at every level

At each CEFR level, you can give more detail and respond more freely when the other person adds new information or sees things differently.

A1

Use short phrases for identity, location, food, time and simple preferences.

A2

Describe a normal day, talk about basic past events, and arrange familiar activities.

B1

Tell a connected story, explain a goal, and solve an everyday problem through conversation.

B2

Support an opinion, compare different views, and adjust your tone and detail in a longer conversation.

Practise Italian in situations you may really meet

Travel

Ask a host how to reach a hill town, change a ferry booking, or explain that your luggage has not arrived.

Work

Study

Describe the focus of a paper, compare lecture notes, or ask a classmate to rehearse part of a presentation.

Restaurants and cafés

Neighbourhood life

Find a local market, explain a maintenance problem, or ask when a community event begins.

Everyday relationships

Make weekend plans, talk about a family recipe, or tell a friend why you enjoyed a place they suggested.

Italian speaking practice questions

Start with a situation you understand, but leave enough room to give your own answer.

Can I start Italian conversation practice at A1?

Yes. Choose a small task such as ordering a drink, giving your name, or asking where something is. Completing a brief conversation gives you a base for longer answers.

How can I work on Italian articles and word endings?

Look at the corrected sentence, notice which words change with the noun, and make a new example with a different noun. A complete sentence makes the connection easier to see.

Should I choose voice or text for Italian?

Use voice when you want to answer more immediately and text when you want to check spelling, articles or verb forms. You can study a pattern in text and then try it by voice.

Can the conversation continue after I make a mistake?

Yes. A correction helps you express what you meant; it does not end the conversation. Notice the change, then answer the next question.

How do guided lessons complement open Italian practice?

A guided lesson gives you a clear topic and goal. An open situation lets you use the same Italian with different people, details and outcomes.
