French for learners who need more chances to respond

A notebook full of vocabulary does not automatically make a restaurant question or a station announcement easy to answer. Regular conversation practice helps you turn familiar French into an answer when you need it.

Use the French you already know

Familiar verbs, linking words and polite phrases become easier to remember when you need them for a real purpose. The situation gives you a starting point, but the answer is your own.

Move beyond prepared phrases

Beginners can practise greetings and simple requests until they feel comfortable. Intermediate learners and students can tell a story, explain a preference, or clear up a misunderstanding.

Prepare for people and places

Travellers can rehearse transport and accommodation. Workers can practise courteous questions and updates. People without regular French conversation practice can create a repeatable routine at home.

Choose the French you want to use today

Select French, an A1, A2, B1 or B2 level, a topic or situation, and an AI tutor. Answer aloud by voice, or type when you want more time to think about your wording.

Begin with a concrete purpose

Ask for a train connection, introduce yourself at a conference, or plan an evening with friends. A clear purpose makes each new word easier to place in context.

Let the exchange develop

The AI French tutor follows the ongoing situation. If you ask about departure time, the next turn can concern the platform, the fare, or a delay instead of resetting to an unrelated question.

See a clearer way to say it

Corrections show how your sentence could sound clearer or more natural. Compare the two versions, make another example, and then carry on with the conversation.

From a ticket request to a useful follow-up

This short French dialogue practises a polite request, useful train vocabulary, and the difference between a single and return journey.

Dialogue : à la gare

Tuteur : Bonjour, je peux vous aider ?

Apprenant : Oui, je voudrais un billet pour Lyon, s’il vous plaît.

Tuteur : Un aller simple ou un aller-retour ?

Apprenant : Un aller-retour pour demain matin.

Correction: choosing a natural adverb

Try again: « J’ai besoin de pratiquer mon français plus. »

More natural: « J’ai besoin de pratiquer davantage le français. »

Here, davantage makes the sentence sound more natural.

Expand what you can do at each French level

CEFR levels change the amount of support, detail and flexibility expected from the conversation, not simply the length of a vocabulary list.

A1

Use names, numbers, dates and memorised polite forms to complete a short everyday task.

A2

Describe a routine, link a few past actions, and ask uncomplicated questions about familiar needs.

B1

Tell a clear story, explain a preference, and find another way to express an idea when a word is missing.

B2

Explain a viewpoint in detail and move more comfortably between formal and relaxed conversations.

Use French in everyday situations

French conversation practice is easier to remember when you know where you are, who you are speaking to, and what you need to do.

Travel

Confirm whether a train requires a reservation, ask a hotel to store luggage, or seek directions after leaving the métro.

Work

Introduce your responsibilities, rearrange a meeting, or explain politely why a delivery date has changed.

Study

Discuss a reading, compare interpretations with a classmate, or ask for a missing detail about an assignment.

Cafés and restaurants

Request a table on the terrace, ask how a dish is prepared, and respond when the server suggests a dessert.

Everyday errands

Collect a parcel, describe what you need at a pharmacy, or ask a shop assistant whether another size is available.

Social conversation

Talk about a neighbourhood, recommend a book, or explain how you spent a holiday without following a fixed script.

Questions about practising French with AI

Use these answers to choose a level, mode and routine that match the French you want to use.

Can a beginner use the French conversations?

Yes. At A1, choose a narrow task such as buying a ticket or giving your name. Short turns and familiar information make it easier to complete an exchange before adding detail.

How can French conversation practice help with articles and adjective endings?

A written correction can show which words need to match in a complete sentence. Build another sentence with the same pattern instead of memorising the correction alone.

Should I answer in French even when I am unsure?

Try the clearest sentence you can form. The value comes from making a choice, seeing a useful alternative, and continuing. Waiting for a perfect answer can reduce the amount of active practice.

Can I switch between voice and text?

Yes. Voice is useful when you want to produce an answer directly; text gives you time to study spelling and sentence shape. Both modes keep the conversation connected to the chosen situation.

What makes a good regular French routine?

Alternate a guided lesson with a real-life scenario, and keep a short list of patterns you want to reuse. One free lesson per day offers a natural prompt to return.
