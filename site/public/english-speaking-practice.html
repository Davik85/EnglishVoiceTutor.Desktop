Turn familiar English into words you can say

English speaking practice is useful when reading and listening feel easier than answering in the moment. The goal is not to recite perfect lines; it is to build the habit of forming a useful response and keeping the exchange moving.

Use more of the English you know

If you know many words but pause when speaking, a familiar situation helps you bring them to mind, combine them, and finish your thought.

For beginners, intermediate learners and students

Beginners can rehearse short everyday exchanges, while intermediate learners and students can explain opinions, compare options, and ask more precise follow-up questions.

For travel and working life

Travellers can prepare for practical encounters. Workers can practise introductions, requests and updates. Anyone who lacks regular conversation practice can return for a short session more often.

How an English conversation session unfolds

A small English conversation can train several skills

This café exchange practises a polite request, a follow-up choice, and a short answer. It is simple enough for an A1 or A2 learner but still feels like a useful conversation.

Dialogue: ordering lunch

Tutor: Good morning. What can I get for you?

Learner: Could I have a flat white and a toasted cheese sandwich, please?

Tutor: Of course. Would you like to eat here or take it away?

Learner: I’ll eat here, thank you.

Correction: expressing duration

Try again: “I have lived here since three years.”

Better: “I have lived here for three years.”

Use for with a length of time. Since introduces the starting point, as in “since 2023.” The correction is specific enough to remember and reuse without stopping the conversation for a long grammar lecture.

Build from direct answers to flexible discussion

The same topic can work differently at each CEFR level. As you improve, you can give more detail, react to surprises, and keep the conversation going for longer.

A1

Exchange names, prices, times and basic preferences with short, supported phrases.

A2

Connect simple sentences to describe routines, past activities and straightforward plans.

B1

Explain reasons, tell a connected story and handle an unexpected turn in a familiar situation.

B2

Compare viewpoints, explain an opinion carefully, and keep a longer conversation moving with more flexible wording.

Practise the English moments that recur in real life

A practical situation gives grammar and vocabulary a purpose. Rotate settings so that familiar phrases become adaptable rather than tied to one memorised script.

Travel

Clarify a platform change, ask whether breakfast is included, or explain a problem with a room reservation.

Work

Give a brief project update, request a revised deadline, or welcome a new colleague to a team call.

Study

Summarise an article, ask a lecturer for clarification, or discuss how to divide a group assignment.

Restaurants and cafés

Ask about ingredients, change a side dish, respond to a server, and settle the bill politely.

Everyday situations

Arrange a repair, return an unsuitable item, speak to a neighbour, or make plans for the weekend.

Everyday chat

Discuss a film, local news, hobbies or personal goals so that speaking English becomes part of your normal routine.

Practise without arranging another person’s calendar

English speaking practice questions

These answers focus on how an English practice session can fit different goals and confidence levels.

Can I practise speaking English if I understand more than I can say?

Yes. Begin with a familiar topic and a suitable CEFR level. Short follow-up questions help you use words you already recognise without asking you to prepare a long speech.

Is the English practice only for beginners?

No. A1 and A2 sessions support everyday exchanges, while B1 and B2 practice can ask you to explain reasons, describe consequences, compare choices and maintain a longer discussion.

Can I use text instead of voice?

Yes. Choose voice when you want to practise forming spoken answers, or text when you want extra time to inspect wording. The conversation continues from the context in either mode.

What should I do with an English correction?

Compare the two sentences, identify the changed pattern, and write or say one new example of your own. Reusing the pattern later in the same topic makes the correction more practical.

How often should I do English conversation practice?

A short, regular session is easier to repeat than an occasional marathon. One free lesson per day provides a simple rhythm, and the guided library offers another topic when you are unsure what to practise.
